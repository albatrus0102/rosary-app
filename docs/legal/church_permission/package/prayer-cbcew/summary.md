# Rosarium — English

**What it is.** “Rosarium” is a free Android app that helps people pray the Rosary and the Stations of the Cross, made by a Korean lay Catholic as an individual.

**Who makes it, and why.** One person, not a company: a long-time Korean Catholic whose whole family is Catholic (their child, baptised as an infant, served at the altar). The app began so that the family could pray the Rosary as they did in the parish they once attended, and to let people who may never visit them pray, at least indirectly, in famous churches. Recently, religious “metaverse” apps have drawn attention, and many Catholics have wished for something like this for the Catholic Church: the author felt the need for an app that keeps reverence and carries the prayers accurately. No revenue at all: the author bears every cost. The hope: more occasions for Catholics to pray the Rosary, and a more approachable Church for those who are not Catholic.

**Free and non-commercial.** No advertising, no paid features, no donations. It is not an official service of the Church and says so.

**How we keep it reverent.** Because this is a place of prayer, reverence is not left to users’ good will alone: it is built into the app as a rule. ① The sanctuary cannot be entered. In every church the altar area is a no-entry zone: the avatar stops at the foot of the steps. Automated tests check this, church by church. ② No imitation of the Mass or the sacraments. Inside the church the app states: “Prayer in a virtual church does not replace attending Mass or the sacraments (Confession, the Eucharist, and so on).” ③ Only prayerful actions. A bow, the sign of the cross, a greeting, sitting or kneeling to pray, lighting a candle. A candle can be lit only at the candle stand; the sign of the cross is offered at the holy water font, where the model has one; a wave of greeting is possible only near the entrance (elsewhere it becomes a bow). There are no games, no combat, no jokes. ④ No chat. Even a candle intention is chosen from fixed, prayerful phrases, with no free text, so no abusive words can appear. ⑤ Faithful prayer texts. The Korean texts were checked word by word against the official prayer book of the Korean bishops; in other languages only the official texts will be used. ⑥ The liturgical calendar. The colour of the altar frontal and the seasonal decorations change with the liturgical season. ⑦ Honest about what it is. Each church carries a notice that it is a simplified virtual model, different from the real church and not endorsed; works of art still in copyright are replaced by abstract forms. ⑧ No advertising, no payments, no selling of data.

**Why we ask you.** Your copyright page (https://liturgyoffice.org/Resources/Copyright/index.shtml) explains that most liturgical texts belong to ICEL and that a permanent publication should ask the rights holder directly; your “Traditional Prayers” page (https://liturgyoffice.org/Prayer/Traditional/index.shtml) is marked © CBCEW. This is my understanding; please correct me if a different procedure applies.

**What we ask.** Permission (or confirmation that none is needed) to show the prayer texts below, unchanged, with your credit line.

a) that you confirm or correct the texts I attach (file “Texts I intend to use”, each with its source), so that I do not publish an unverified version; where I have marked a text as not verified, please provide or confirm the form approved for use in England and Wales;
b) whether the traditional prayers listed on your “Traditional Prayers” page (the Lord’s Prayer, the Hail Mary, the Glory Be, the Apostles’ Creed, the Hail Holy Queen, the Rosary) may be shown in a free app;
c) which of them are your copyright, ICEL’s, or the Catholic Truth Society’s (the Compendium), and whom I should ask for each;
d) where the wording used in England and Wales differs from that used in the United States, and whether the same texts serve for Ireland;
e) what credit line you require.

**How the texts are used.**

- Shown as text on the screen, unchanged, with the credit line you specify, on the prayer screen and on a “Prayer sources” page.
- Text only. English prayers are never read aloud by the app: no recorded voice, no synthetic (AI) voice, no text-to-speech. (Only the Korean edition has a voice, under a separate request to the Korean bishops’ conference.)
- For praying in two parts, each prayer is shown as a leader’s part and a response; nothing is altered, and the two parts together are the complete text. An optional “short view” shows only the opening words of prayers the user knows by heart.
- The app contains no Scripture text (references only), no Mass texts and no hymns.
- A free app on Google Play, available worldwide.

**Your answer (one number is enough).**

☐ ① No objection — permission as described (credit line, if any: ______).
☐ ② Permission with conditions or corrections — please tell us: ______
☐ ③ Please do not use our text (we will not use it).

The texts themselves are in the attachment “Texts I intend to use”, each with its source. If you wish to be more specific, the detailed items D1, D2 … are listed below, at point 3.

**Without an answer.** If I receive no answer, I will not use any text in which you hold the copyright. Users in Great Britain and Ireland will then see the same public-domain traditional texts as the general English edition, with their source shown. If you grant permission, the app will state the scope of that permission inside the app, and I will not add anything beyond it that could cause misunderstanding. If you wish, I will gladly ask persons you designate to review the app.

### Who I am, and why I made this app

The app is being prepared in English, with a separate text file for users in Great Britain and Ireland. I do not want to translate the prayers myself from Korean: I want to show, word for word, the texts that the Church in England and Wales actually prays. I am writing to ask what I may use, and on what terms. If this request belongs to another office, I would be grateful if you would forward it or tell me whom to write to.

I am not a company but one person. I have been a Catholic for many years and my whole family is Catholic; our child was baptised as an infant and served at the altar. The app began at home: my family asked to be able to pray the Rosary the way we used to pray it in the parish we once attended. Along the way I also wished to let people experience, at least indirectly, praying the Rosary in famous churches they may never be able to visit. Recently, religious “metaverse” apps have drawn attention, and I have seen many Catholics say they wish there were something like this for the Catholic Church. I felt the need for an app that keeps reverence and carries the prayers accurately, so I decided to make one with care.

There is no revenue model at all: no advertising, no paid features; I bear all the costs of the service myself, and it is completely free. It began from a simple wish to help many of the faithful. In the process I learned that a number of elements require permission, so I am writing, one by one, to each church, diocese and rights holder concerned. For now I have left out everything that I judged could raise a copyright issue. If, to deepen Catholic devotional life and to help the wider public take an interest in the Church, you are willing to permit the use of certain protected texts, I will include them (see the options). My hope is that Catholics will have more occasions to pray the Rosary, and that those who are not Catholic may find the Church more approachable.
